## Completeness audit

1. Domain: every positive n has a well-defined positive F(n). No orbit
   enters zero. The artificial zero case is expressly excluded.
2. Boundary: n=60000 is checked in the certificate; the analytic argument
   applies to every n>60000. There is no gap between the ranges.
3. Bound: e<3 and exact 3^10=59049<60000 imply ln(n)>10 for n>60000;
   Rosser's theorem is unconditional and has the needed one-based index.
4. Decimal bridge: a prime has nonzero last digit, so reversal loses no
   digit. An input with d digits maps, above the threshold, to a number
   with at least d+1 digits. No size-monotonicity of reversal is assumed.
5. Finite completeness: all 60000 indices are present once. Independent
   sieve verification establishes the complete prime list and its order,
   and independent string reversal establishes every transition.
6. Exit certificate: positive ranks decrease by exactly one on each
   internal edge, and rank one exits. Rank induction covers all finite
   inputs and supplies the verified maximum of ten steps.
7. Independent cycle algorithm: Kahn elimination removes all 60000
   vertices without reading ranks. A directed cycle would leave each of
   its vertices with an incoming edge and so could not be eliminated.
8. Infinite conclusion: the region above 60000 is invariant and strictly
   increasing. Integer strict increase implies divergence and contradicts
   any eventual positive period. An alternative maximum-on-cycle proof
   was also checked in PROOF.md.
9. Reproducibility: clean_replay.py rebuilt in a fresh temporary directory,
   reran the independent checker, and obtained byte-identical certificate
   and matching verification JSON. Versions and exact commands are saved
   in clean_replay.json. This is a clean directory, not a separate machine.

No unresolved mathematical obligation was found in this audit. Verification
consists of an ordinary proof using an established theorem, independently
implemented exact finite checks, and same-model review of the bridges.
The full theorem has not been Lean-kernel checked or externally reviewed.
